## Why Small Businesses Buy Cyber

Roughly half of cyber claims come from businesses under 100 employees.
Ransomware, business email compromise, and vendor credential theft dominate
small-claim activity. A standalone cyber policy covers first-party response
costs (forensics, notification, credit monitoring, business interruption) and
third-party liability (defense, settlements, regulatory fines where insurable).

## Security Requirements

Most carriers require, at minimum: multi-factor authentication on email and
remote access, offsite or immutable backups, and employee security awareness
training. Failure to maintain these at renewal can trigger a mid-term
condition endorsement.

## Coverage Triggers

First-party coverage typically triggers on a "security failure" — unauthorized
access to the insured's network or data. Social engineering fraud (funds
transfer loss) is often written as a separate sublimit and can be subject to a
60-90 day discovery period.

## Industry-Specific Gaps

Healthcare providers must pair cyber with HIPAA breach response. Retailers
handling card data need PCI DSS compliance attestation or face coverage
denials on payment-related incidents. Professional services firms often
underinsure third-party liability because they confuse E&O with cyber.
